Topic 5: Solution Stoichiometry

1 Core Concept — 30 Seconds

▼
🔑 One-Line Summary
Solution stoichiometry applies stoichiometric calculations to substances in solution, using molarity (c = n/V) to convert between volume and moles.
c = n / V   →   n = c × V   →   V = n / c
🧠 Key Vocabulary
Solvent: dissolves the other substance (usually larger quantity)
Solute: dissolves in the solvent (usually smaller quantity)
Molarity (c or M): moles of solute per litre of solution (mol/L)
Vol A → (× cA) → Mol A → (× mole ratio) → Mol B → (÷ cB) → Vol B

2 Problem-Solving Steps

▼
📋 Steps for Solution Stoichiometry
Write and balance the chemical equation
Calculate moles of given substance: n = c × V
(Make sure volume is in litres!)
Use the mole ratio to find moles of required substance
Convert to the required quantity:
• Volume: V = n / c
• Concentration: c = n / V
• Mass: m = n × M
⚠️ Most Common Mistake
Convert mL to L before calculating!
1 mL = 0.001 L → multiply mL by 0.001 or divide by 1000
Also: 1 kL = 1000 L
📌 Typical Chem 20 Reactions
• Precipitation reactions (insoluble product forms)
• Acid-base neutralization reactions

3 Must-Know Problem — Full Worked Example

▼
📝 H₂SO₄ Concentration from KOH Titration

Given: 10.00 mL H₂SO₄ is neutralized by 15.9 mL of 0.150 mol/L KOH. Find [H₂SO₄].

Balanced equation:

H₂SO₄(aq) + 2 KOH(aq) → 2 H₂O(ℓ) + K₂SO₄(aq)

Convert mL → L:

VH₂SO₄ = 0.01000 L | VKOH = 0.0159 L

One-line calculation:

[H₂SO₄] = VKOH × cKOH × (mole ratio) / VH₂SO₄

[H₂SO₄] = 0.0159 L × (0.150 mol/L) × (1 mol H₂SO₄ / 2 mol KOH) / 0.01000 L
[H₂SO₄] = 0.119 mol/L (3 sig digs)
📝 Ammonia + Phosphoric Acid Volume

Given: What volume of 14.8 mol/L NH₃ reacts with 1.00 kL of 12.9 mol/L H₃PO₄?

NH₃(aq) + H₃PO₄(aq) → NH₄H₂PO₄(s)
VNH₃ = 1.00 × 10³ L × (12.9 mol/L) × (1 mol NH₃ / 1 mol H₃PO₄) × (L / 14.8 mol)
VNH₃ = 872 L = 0.872 kL (3 sig digs)

4 Quick Check — 3 Questions

▼

Q1: To convert volume of solution to moles of solute, you multiply by:

✅ Correct! n = c × V → volume × molarity = moles
❌ n = c × V. Multiply volume (in L) by molarity (mol/L) to get moles.

Q2: 50.0 mL of 0.500 mol/L HCl contains how many moles?

✅ Correct! n = 0.0500 L × 0.500 mol/L = 0.0250 mol
❌ Convert mL to L first! n = 0.0500 L × 0.500 mol/L = 0.0250 mol

Q3: H₂SO₄ + 2 KOH → K₂SO₄ + 2 H₂O
Why is the mole ratio important here?

✅ Correct! Without the correct mole ratio (1:2), your calculation will be wrong by a factor of 2.
❌ The ratio is 1:2 (not 1:1). Always check the balanced equation for the correct ratio!

5 Practice Problem — Challenge Yourself

▼

🎯 Challenge: Ammonium Sulfate Fertilizer

H₂SO₄(aq) + 2 NH₃(aq) → (NH₄)₂SO₄(aq)

50.0 mL of H₂SO₄ reacts with 24.4 mL of 2.20 mol/L NH₃. Calculate [H₂SO₄].

Calculation:
[H₂SO₄] = VNH₃ × cNH₃ × (mole ratio) / VH₂SO₄

[H₂SO₄] = 0.0244 L × (2.20 mol/L) × (1 mol H₂SO₄ / 2 mol NH₃) / 0.0500 L
[H₂SO₄] = 0.537 mol/L (3 sig digs)

🎯 Bonus: Slaked Lime + Aluminum Sulfate

3 Ca(OH)₂(aq) + Al₂(SO₄)₃(aq) → 2 Al(OH)₃(s) + 3 CaSO₄(s)

What volume of 0.0250 mol/L Ca(OH)₂ is needed to react with 25.0 mL of 0.125 mol/L Al₂(SO₄)₃?

VCa(OH)₂ = VAl₂(SO₄)₃ × cAl₂(SO₄)₃ × (mole ratio) / cCa(OH)₂

V = 0.0250 L × (0.125 mol/L) × (3 mol Ca(OH)₂ / 1 mol Al₂(SO₄)₃) / (0.0250 mol/L)
V = 0.375 L = 375 mL (3 sig digs)

📋 Topic 5 Cheat Sheet

Key Formulas
c = n / V (molarity)
n = c × V (moles from solution)
V = n / c (volume needed)
The Flow
Vol A → (×c) → Mol A
→ (ratio) → Mol B
→ (÷c or ×M) → Vol B or Mass B
Remember:
Always convert mL → L before calculating! (÷ 1000)
