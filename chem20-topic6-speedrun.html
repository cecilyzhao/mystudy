Topic 6: Titration Analysis

Chem 20 Stoichiometry — Exam Speed Review
⏱️ ~10 min
0 / 5 complete

1 Core Concept — 30 Seconds

▼
🔑 One-Line Summary
A titration determines an unknown concentration by reacting it with a solution of known concentration (the standard solution / titrant).
🧠 Key Vocabulary
Titrant: solution of known concentration (in the burette)
Standard solution: precisely known concentration (usually strong acid or base)
Indicator: chemical that provides a visual colour change
Endpoint: point where indicator changes colour
Equivalence point: reaction is stoichiometrically complete — equal mol amounts consumed
pH = −log[H₃O⁺]   |   pOH = −log[OH⁻]   |   pH + pOH = 14.00
📌 Sig Dig Rule for pH
# sig digs in concentration = # decimal places in pH (and vice versa)
Example: [H₃O⁺] = 3.76 × 10⁻⁸ (3 sig digs) → pH = 7.425 (3 decimal places)

2 Problem-Solving Steps & Multiple Trials

▼
📋 Steps for Titration Calculations
Write the balanced equation
Calculate Vtitrant = Vfinal − Vinitial (burette readings)
Tabulate known volumes and concentrations
Use solution stoichiometry to find unknown concentration:
cunknown = Vknown × cknown × (mole ratio) / Vunknown
⚠️ Multiple Trials — Must Know for Exams
• Want 2–3 trials within 0.2 mL of each other
• First trial is often done quickly → overshoots → discard as outlier
• Discard outliers, then average the remaining titrant volumes
• Use the average volume in your calculation
🧠 pH After Mixing (Excess Problem)
If asked for pH after mixing two solutions:
1. Calculate n of both acid and base
2. Determine which is limiting (leftover reagent sets pH)
3. Final concentration = leftover moles / TOTAL volume
4. Calculate pH from leftover [H₃O⁺] or [OH⁻]

3 Must-Know Problem — Full Worked Example

▼
📝 HCl Titrated with NaOH

Given: 20.00 mL HCl titrated with 0.150 mol/L NaOH. Burette: initial 1.50 mL, final 29.51 mL.

Balanced equation:

HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → H₂O(ℓ) + NaCl(aq)

Step 1: Volume of titrant

VNaOH = 29.51 − 1.50 = 28.01 mL = 0.02801 L

Step 2: Calculate [HCl]

[HCl] = VNaOH × cNaOH × (1 mol HCl / 1 mol NaOH) / VHCl
[HCl] = 0.02801 × 0.150 × (1/1) / 0.02000
[HCl] = 0.210 mol/L (3 sig digs)
📝 pH After Mixing: 49.00 mL CsOH + 50.00 mL HCl

Given: 49.00 mL of 0.100 M CsOH + 50.00 mL of 0.100 M HCl. Find pH.

nHCl = 0.05000 × 0.100 = 0.00500 mol
nCsOH = 0.04900 × 0.100 = 0.00490 mol

CsOH is limiting → HCl is in excess
nHCl leftover = 0.00500 − 0.00490 = 0.00010 mol

[H₃O⁺] = 0.00010 / (0.05000 + 0.04900) = 0.0010 mol/L
pH = −log(0.0010) = 3.00

4 Quick Check — 3 Questions

▼

Q1: What is the difference between endpoint and equivalence point?

✅ Correct! Endpoint is the visual observation; equivalence point is the theoretical stoichiometric point.
❌ Endpoint = where indicator changes colour. Equivalence point = where reaction is stoichiometrically complete.

Q2: [H₃O⁺] = 3.76 × 10⁻⁶ mol/L. What is the pH?

✅ Correct! pH = −log(3.76 × 10⁻⁶) = 5.425 (3 decimal places for 3 sig digs)
❌ pH = −log(3.76 × 10⁻⁶) = 5.425. Remember: sig digs in concentration = decimal places in pH.

Q3: Trial volumes: 12.50 mL, 12.24 mL, 12.23 mL. What volume should you use?

✅ Correct! Trial 1 is an outlier (>0.2 mL from others). Average the consistent trials: (12.24 + 12.23) / 2 = 12.24 mL
❌ Trial 1 (12.50) is >0.2 mL from the others — discard it! Average 12.24 + 12.23 = 12.24 mL

5 Practice Problem — Challenge Yourself

▼

🎯 Challenge: NaOH + H₂SO₄ Titration

H₂SO₄(aq) + 2 NaOH(aq) → Na₂SO₄(aq) + 2 H₂O(ℓ)

38 mL of 0.75 M NaOH neutralizes 155 mL of H₂SO₄. What is [H₂SO₄]?

[H₂SO₄] = VNaOH × cNaOH × (1 mol H₂SO₄ / 2 mol NaOH) / VH₂SO₄

[H₂SO₄] = 0.038 L × 0.75 mol/L × (1/2) / 0.155 L
[H₂SO₄] = 0.092 mol/L (2 sig digs)

🎯 Bonus: Mixed Solutions pH

25.0 mL of 0.525 M NaOH + 75.0 mL of 0.355 M HCl are mixed.

a) Is the resulting solution acidic or basic?

b) What is the pH?

nNaOH = 0.0250 × 0.525 = 0.01313 mol
nHCl = 0.0750 × 0.355 = 0.02663 mol

HCl excess = 0.02663 − 0.01313 = 0.01350 mol
Total volume = 0.0250 + 0.0750 = 0.1000 L
[H₃O⁺] = 0.01350 / 0.1000 = 0.135 mol/L
pH = −log(0.135) = 0.870 → Acidic
