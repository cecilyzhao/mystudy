1 Core Concept — 30 Seconds

▼
🔑 One-Line Summary
Gravimetric stoichiometry converts mass of a given substance to mass of a required substance through the bridge of moles.
Grams A → (÷ MA) → Moles A → (× mole ratio) → Moles B → (× MB) → Grams B
🧠 Gravi = Mass, Metric = Measurement
In the lab we measure mass, but balanced equations work in moles.
Molar mass (M) is the bridge between mass and moles:
n = m / M and m = n × M

2 4-Step Method — Follow This on Exams

▼
📋 Steps for Gravimetric Stoichiometry
Write the balanced equation and calculate molar masses (Mgiven and Mrequired)
Convert mass → moles of the given substance
ngiven = mgiven / Mgiven
Use the mole ratio to find moles of required substance
nrequired = ngiven × (coeffreq / coeffgiven)
Convert moles → mass of the required substance
mrequired = nrequired × Mrequired
⚠️ Pro Tip: Unit Analysis (One-Line Method)
Steps 2–4 can be combined into a single calculation:
mreq = mgiven × (1/Mgiven) × (mole ratio) × Mreq
Use subscripts to track which chemical species each value belongs to!

3 Must-Know Problem — Full Worked Example

▼
📝 Malachite Decomposition: 1.00 g → ? g CuO

Given: 1.00 g of Cu₂(CO₃)(OH)₂ decomposes. What mass of CuO is formed?

Balanced equation:

Cu₂(CO₃)(OH)₂(s) → 2 CuO(s) + CO₂(g) + H₂O(g)

Step 1: Molar Masses

M(Cu₂(CO₃)(OH)₂) = 221.13 g/mol
M(CuO) = 79.55 g/mol

Steps 2–4: One-line calculation

mCuO = 1.00 g × (mol / 221.13 g) × (2 mol CuO / 1 mol malachite) × (79.55 g / mol CuO)
mCuO = 0.719 g (3 sig digs)
📝 Iron(III) Oxide Reduction: 100.0 g Fe → ? g Fe₂O₃

Given: What mass of Fe₂O₃ is required to produce 100.0 g of iron?

Fe₂O₃(s) + 3 CO(g) → 2 Fe(s) + 3 CO₂(g)

Molar Masses:

M(Fe₂O₃) = 159.70 g/mol | M(Fe) = 55.85 g/mol

Calculation:

mFe₂O₃ = 100.0 g × (mol Fe / 55.85 g) × (1 mol Fe₂O₃ / 2 mol Fe) × (159.70 g / mol Fe₂O₃)
mFe₂O₃ = 143.0 g (4 sig digs)

4 Quick Check — 3 Questions

▼

Q1: In gravimetric stoichiometry, the "bridge" between mass and mass is:

✅ Correct! mass → moles → mole ratio → moles → mass
❌ The bridge is moles! You convert mass to moles, use mole ratio, then back to mass.

Q2: To convert grams to moles, you use:

✅ Correct! n = m / M converts grams to moles.
❌ To go from grams to moles, divide: n = m / M

Q3: C₃H₈ + 5 O₂ → 3 CO₂ + 4 H₂O
Mass of O₂ needed to burn 10.0 g propane?
(M: C₃H₈ = 44.11, O₂ = 32.00)

✅ Correct! 10.0 × (1/44.11) × (5/1) × 32.00 = 36.3 g
❌ Calculate: 10.0 g ÷ 44.11 × (5/1) × 32.00 = 36.3 g O₂

5 Practice Problem — Challenge Yourself

▼

🎯 Challenge: Astronaut CO₂ Removal

2 LiOH(s) + CO₂(g) → Li₂CO₃(s) + H₂O(g)

What mass of LiOH is required to remove 1.00 × 10³ g of CO₂?

Molar Masses:
M(LiOH) = 23.95 g/mol | M(CO₂) = 44.01 g/mol

Calculation:
mLiOH = 1.00 × 10³ g × (mol CO₂ / 44.01 g) × (2 mol LiOH / 1 mol CO₂) × (23.95 g / mol LiOH)
mLiOH = 1.09 × 10³ g = 1.09 kg (3 sig digs)

🎯 Bonus: Chrome Yellow Pigment

Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) + K₂CrO₄(aq) → PbCrO₄(s) + 2 KNO₃(aq)

What mass of PbCrO₄ is produced from 5.10 g of Pb(NO₃)₂?

M(Pb(NO₃)₂) = 331.2 g/mol | M(PbCrO₄) = 323.2 g/mol

mPbCrO₄ = 5.10 g × (mol / 331.2 g) × (1/1) × (323.2 g/mol)
mPbCrO₄ = 4.98 g (3 sig digs)

★ Note: Compounds containing lead have molar masses with only 1 decimal place due to the lower precision of lead's atomic mass.

📋 Topic 2 Cheat Sheet

Key Formulas
n = m / M (mass → moles)
m = n × M (moles → mass)
Mole ratio from balanced equation
The Flow
g A → mol A → mol B → g B
(÷M) → (ratio) → (×M)
Can combine into one line!
Conservation of Mass:
Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products (always!)
