Topic 4: Limiting & Excess Reagents

Chem 20 Stoichiometry — Exam Speed Review
⏱️ ~10 min
0 / 5 完成

1 Core Concept — 30 Seconds

▼
🔑 One-Line Summary
When two reactants are mixed, the one that runs out first is the Limiting Reagent (LR).
The one left over is the Excess Reagent.
🧠 Memory Trick — Hot Dog Analogy
12 sausages + 8 buns → can only make 8 hot dogs
Buns run out first = Limiting Reagent
4 leftover sausages = Excess Reagent
8 assembled hot dogs = Theoretical Yield
Limiting Reagent → the reactant that produces the LEAST amount of product

2 Problem-Solving Steps — Follow This on Exams

▼
📋 4-Step Method (Must show on exams!)
Balance the equation + calculate Molar Masses
Convert both reactants to moles
n = m / M
Scenario Test: Assume A is LR → calculate how much B is needed → do you have enough?
Assume B is LR → calculate how much A is needed → do you have enough?
Whichever you DON'T have enough of = LR
Use the LR's moles to calculate mass of product
m = n × mole ratio × M
⚠️ Most Common Exam Mistake
ALWAYS calculate product from the Limiting Reagent! Using the Excess Reagent gives the WRONG answer.

To find leftover excess:
leftover = total excess moles − moles consumed by LR

3 Must-Know Problem — Full Worked Example

▼
📝 Classic Problem: 10.0 g Cu + 20.0 g AgNO₃ → Ag = ?

Given: 10.0 g Cu, 20.0 g AgNO₃

Balanced equation:

Cu(s) + 2 AgNO₃(aq) → 2 Ag(s) + Cu(NO₃)₂(aq)

Step 1: Molar Masses

M(Cu) = 63.55 g/mol
M(AgNO₃) = 107.87 + 14.01 + 3(16.00) = 169.88 g/mol
M(Ag) = 107.87 g/mol

Step 2: Convert to moles

n(Cu) = 10.0 / 63.55 = 0.1574 mol
n(AgNO₃) = 20.0 / 169.88 = 0.1177 mol

Step 3: Scenario Test

Scenario A: Cu is LR?
Need: 0.1574 mol Cu × (2 mol AgNO₃ / 1 mol Cu) = 0.3148 mol AgNO₃
Have: 0.1177 mol ← NOT ENOUGH! ✗

Scenario B: AgNO₃ is LR?
Need: 0.1177 mol AgNO₃ × (1 mol Cu / 2 mol AgNO₃) = 0.0589 mol Cu
Have: 0.1574 mol ← ENOUGH ✓

∴ AgNO₃ is Limiting, Cu is Excess

Step 4: Calculate product from LR

m(Ag) = 0.1177 mol AgNO₃ × (2 mol Ag / 2 mol AgNO₃) × 107.87 g/mol
m(Ag) = 12.7 g (3 sig digs)

Bonus: Leftover Cu

n(Cu used) = 0.1177 × (1/2) = 0.0589 mol
n(Cu leftover) = 0.1574 − 0.0589 = 0.0986 mol
m(Cu leftover) = 0.0986 × 63.55 = 6.26 g

4 Quick Check — 3 Questions

▼

Q1: 2.20 mol Mg + 3.30 mol HCl
Mg(s) + 2 HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)
Which is limiting?

✅ Correct! HCl is limiting. Need 2.20×2 = 4.40 mol HCl but only have 3.30.
❌ Try again. Calculate: if Mg is LR, how much HCl do you need?

Q2: Which reagent do you use to calculate theoretical yield?

✅ Correct! Always calculate product from the Limiting Reagent.
❌ Nope — using excess reagent gives the WRONG answer. Always use LR!

Q3: 10.0 mol CH₄ + 10.0 mol O₂
CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O
How much CO₂ (mol) is produced?

✅ Correct! O₂ is limiting (need 20 mol for 10 mol CH₄, only have 10). From LR: 10.0 mol O₂ × (1 CO₂ / 2 O₂) = 5.0 mol CO₂
❌ O₂ is limiting! 10 mol O₂ × (1 mol CO₂ / 2 mol O₂) = 5.0 mol

5 Practice Problem — Challenge Yourself

▼

🎯 Challenge: 3 NH₄NO₃ + Na₃PO₄ → (NH₄)₃PO₄ + 3 NaNO₃

Starting with 30.0 g ammonium nitrate and 50.0 g sodium phosphate:

a) Which reagent is limiting?

b) What is the maximum mass of (NH₄)₃PO₄?

c) How much excess reagent is left over?

Molar Masses:
M(NH₄NO₃) = 80.06 g/mol
M(Na₃PO₄) = 163.94 g/mol
M((NH₄)₃PO₄) = 149.12 g/mol

Moles:
n(NH₄NO₃) = 30.0 / 80.06 = 0.3747 mol
n(Na₃PO₄) = 50.0 / 163.94 = 0.3050 mol

Scenario Test:
If NH₄NO₃ is LR: need 0.3747 × (1/3) = 0.1249 mol Na₃PO₄ ← have 0.3050 ✓
If Na₃PO₄ is LR: need 0.3050 × 3 = 0.9149 mol NH₄NO₃ ← have 0.3747 ✗

∴ NH₄NO₃ is LIMITING

b) Max product:
m = 0.3747 × (1/3) × 149.12 = 18.6 g (NH₄)₃PO₄

c) Excess leftover:
n(Na₃PO₄ used) = 0.3747 × (1/3) = 0.1249 mol
n(leftover) = 0.3050 − 0.1249 = 0.1801 mol
m(leftover) = 0.1801 × 163.94 = 29.5 g Na₃PO₄

🎯 Bonus: With Percent Yield

35 g C₆H₁₀ + 45 g O₂

2 C₆H₁₀ + 17 O₂ → 12 CO₂ + 10 H₂O

a) How many grams of CO₂ formed?

b) Which is limiting?

c) If actual CO₂ = 35 g, what is % yield?

M(C₆H₁₀) = 82.16 g/mol | M(O₂) = 32.00 g/mol | M(CO₂) = 44.01 g/mol

n(C₆H₁₀) = 35 / 82.16 = 0.4261 mol
n(O₂) = 45 / 32.00 = 1.406 mol

If C₆H₁₀ is LR: need 0.4261 × (17/2) = 3.622 mol O₂ ← only have 1.406 ✗
If O₂ is LR: need 1.406 × (2/17) = 0.1654 mol C₆H₁₀ ← have 0.4261 ✓

∴ O₂ is LIMITING

m(CO₂) = 1.406 × (12/17) × 44.01 = 43.7 g

% yield = (35 / 43.7) × 100% = 80.1%

📋 Topic 4 Cheat Sheet

Limiting Reagent
Completely consumed
Produces LESS product
Use it to calculate theoretical yield
Excess Reagent
Has leftovers
leftover = total − used
Do NOT use it to calculate product
Solving Mantra:
Both to moles → Scenario test → Not enough = LR → Calculate product from LR
