Topic 3: Percent Yield

0 / 5 complete

1 Core Concept — 30 Seconds

▼
🔑 One-Line Summary
Percent yield compares the actual yield (from lab) to the theoretical yield (from stoichiometry) to evaluate how well a reaction performed.
% yield = (actual yield / theoretical yield) × 100%
🧠 Where Do the Values Come From?
Actual yield = measured in the lab (experimental data)
Theoretical yield = calculated from stoichiometry (the math)
For school labs, 5–10% total uncertainty is typical; ≥90% yield is often acceptable.
📌 Applications
• Test experimental designs and technological skills
• Evaluate purity of chemicals
• Assess the quantitative nature of a reaction

2 Steps & Experimental Uncertainties

▼
📋 Steps for Percent Yield Problems
Calculate the theoretical yield using gravimetric stoichiometry
(mass → mol → ratio → mol → mass)
Identify the actual yield from the lab data
Apply the formula: % yield = (actual / theoretical) × 100%
⚠️ Common Sources of Experimental Uncertainty
• All equipment has inherent limitations
• Initial values derived from measurements
• Purity of the grade of chemical used
• Washing a precipitate can lose fine particles
• Qualitative judgements (e.g., estimation of colour changes)
🧠 Reverse Problem
If given % yield and theoretical yield, find actual yield:
mactual = (% yield × mtheoretical) / 100%

3 Must-Know Problem — Full Worked Example

▼
📝 Silver Chromate Precipitation: 3.00 g AgNO₃ → 2.81 g precipitate

Given: 3.00 g AgNO₃ + excess Na₂CrO₄ → 2.81 g precipitate. Calculate % yield.

Balanced equation:

2 AgNO₃(aq) + Na₂CrO₄(aq) → 2 NaNO₃(aq) + Ag₂CrO₄(s)

Step 1: Theoretical yield (stoichiometry)

M(AgNO₃) = 169.88 g/mol | M(Ag₂CrO₄) = 331.74 g/mol

mtheor = 3.00 g × (mol / 169.88 g) × (1 mol Ag₂CrO₄ / 2 mol AgNO₃) × (331.74 g/mol)
mtheor = 2.93 g

Step 2: Actual yield = 2.81 g (from lab)

Step 3: Percent yield

% yield = (2.81 / 2.93) × 100%
% yield = 95.9% (3 sig digs)

★ Use the unrounded theoretical value in the % yield calculation

4 Quick Check — 3 Questions

▼

Q1: Where does the "actual yield" value come from?

✅ Correct! Actual yield is measured experimentally in the lab.
❌ Actual = lab measurement. Theoretical = stoichiometry calculation.

Q2: Theoretical yield = 6.11 g, actual yield = 5.81 g. What is the % yield?

✅ Correct! (5.81 / 6.11) × 100% = 95.1%
❌ % yield = (5.81 / 6.11) × 100% = 95.1%

Q3: If percent yield is greater than 100%, this most likely means:

✅ Correct! >100% yield indicates experimental error — product may contain impurities or water.
❌ Yields above 100% indicate experimental error, not a super-efficient reaction!

5 Practice Problem — Challenge Yourself

▼

🎯 Challenge: Strontium Sulfate Precipitation

Sr(NO₃)₂(aq) + CuSO₄(aq) → SrSO₄(s) + Cu(NO₃)₂(aq)

1.84 g of strontium nitrate produces 1.50 g of strontium sulfate. Calculate the % yield.

Molar Masses:
M(Sr(NO₃)₂) = 211.64 g/mol | M(SrSO₄) = 183.69 g/mol

Theoretical yield:
mtheor = 1.84 g × (mol / 211.64 g) × (1/1) × (183.69 g/mol) = 1.60 g

% yield:
% yield = (1.50 / 1.60) × 100% = 93.9% (3 sig digs)

🎯 Bonus: Copper(II) Hydroxide (Reverse Problem)

2 KOH(aq) + Cu(NO₃)₂(aq) → Cu(OH)₂(s) + 2 KNO₃(aq)

2.67 g of KOH reacts with excess Cu(NO₃)₂. If the percent yield is 75.4%, what is the actual mass of Cu(OH)₂ produced?

M(KOH) = 56.11 g/mol | M(Cu(OH)₂) = 97.57 g/mol

Theoretical yield:
mtheor = 2.67 g × (mol / 56.11 g) × (1 mol Cu(OH)₂ / 2 mol KOH) × (97.57 g/mol) = 2.32 g

Actual yield:
mactual = (75.4% × 2.32 g) / 100% = 1.75 g (3 sig digs)

📋 Topic 3 Cheat Sheet

The Formula
% yield = (actual / theoretical) × 100%
Actual → from lab
Theoretical → from stoichiometry
Uncertainty Sources
Equipment limitations
Chemical purity
Washing precipitate losses
Judgement calls (colour changes)
Reverse Formula:
mactual = (% yield × mtheoretical) / 100%
