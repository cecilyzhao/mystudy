1 Core Concept — 30 Seconds

▼
🔑 One-Line Summary
A pH titration curve is an S-shaped plot of pH vs. volume of titrant. The equivalence point is at the middle of the steep section, and the right indicator must change colour within that steep region.
4 Curve Types: SA+SB (pH 7)  |  WA+SB (pH > 7)  |  SA+WB (pH < 7)  |  WA+WB (indistinct)
🧠 Equivalence Point pH — Must Memorize
Strong Acid + Strong Base: equivalence at pH = 7
Weak Acid + Strong Base: equivalence at pH > 7 (conjugate base is basic)
Strong Acid + Weak Base: equivalence at pH < 7 (conjugate acid is acidic)
Weak Acid + Weak Base: equivalence point is indistinct (hard to determine)

2 How to Read & Draw Titration Curves

▼
📋 Drawing a Titration Curve Checklist
Calculate/estimate starting pH (pH of solution in flask)
Calculate/estimate ending pH (pH approaches titrant's pH)
Determine equivalence point pH (7 for SA+SB, >7 for WA+SB, <7 for SA+WB)
Calculate volume at equivalence if given enough info:
Veq = Vsample × csample × (mole ratio) / ctitrant
Draw S-shaped curve with steep section at equivalence point
Label axes: pH (y) vs. Volume of Titrant (x)
⚠️ Choosing the Right Indicator
The indicator's colour change range must fall within the steep section of the curve.

SA + SB (equiv pH 7): bromothymol blue (6.0–7.6) ✓
WA + SB (equiv pH > 7): phenolphthalein (8.2–10.0) ✓
SA + WB (equiv pH < 7): methyl red (4.8–6.0) or methyl orange (3.2–4.4) ✓
📌 Reading a Curve
• Starts low → acid in flask, base in burette (or vice versa)
• Steep section = near equivalence point (small volume → large pH change)
• After steep section, pH levels off toward titrant's pH
• Weak acid/base curves have a less steep section (less distinct)

3 Must-Know Problem — Full Worked Example

▼
📝 25 mL of 0.10 M NaOH titrated with 0.20 M HCl

Determine: initial pH, equivalence volume, equivalence pH, and best indicator.

What's in the flask?

NaOH (strong base) → titrated with HCl (strong acid)
This is a Strong Base + Strong Acid titration

Initial pH:

[OH⁻] = 0.10 M → pOH = −log(0.10) = 1.00
pH = 14.00 − 1.00 = 13.00

Volume at equivalence:

Veq = VNaOH × cNaOH / cHCl
Veq = 0.025 L × 0.10 / 0.20 = 0.0125 L = 12.5 mL HCl

Equivalence point pH:

SA + SB → equivalence at pH = 7

Best indicator:

Bromothymol blue (6.0–7.6) — changes colour right at pH 7

Curve shape: Starts at pH 13 → drops through steep section at pH 7 (12.5 mL) → levels off at low pH

4 Quick Check — 3 Questions

▼

Q1: A curve starts at pH 2 and rises to pH 12 with equivalence at pH 7. What was in the flask?

✅ Correct! Starts low (acid), rises (base added), equiv at pH 7 = SA + SB.
❌ pH starts low (acid in flask), rises as base is added. Equiv at pH 7 = both strong.

Q2: Best indicator for SA + SB titration (equivalence at pH 7)?

✅ Correct! Bromothymol blue changes colour right at pH 7 — perfect for SA+SB.
❌ The indicator must change colour within the steep section (near pH 7). Bromothymol blue (6.0–7.6) is the best choice.

Q3: Weak acid titrated with strong base. The equivalence point pH is:

✅ Correct! WA + SB → conjugate base is produced → solution is basic → pH > 7 at equivalence.
❌ Weak acid + strong base: the conjugate base at equivalence is basic, so pH > 7.

5 Practice Problem — Challenge Yourself

▼

🎯 Challenge: HNO₃ Titrated with KOH

20.0 mL of 0.030 M HNO₃ titrated with 0.040 M KOH.

a) Initial pH?

b) Volume of KOH at equivalence?

c) pH at equivalence?

d) Sketch description of the curve.

a) Initial pH:
pH = −log(0.030) = 1.52

b) Volume at equivalence:
Veq = 0.0200 L × 0.030 / 0.040 = 0.015 L = 15 mL KOH

c) pH at equivalence:
SA + SB → pH = 7

d) Curve: Starts at pH ~1.5, flat initially, steep S-rise through pH 7 at 15 mL, levels off at pH ~12–13. Best indicator: bromothymol blue.

🎯 Bonus: Mg(OH)₂ Titrated with HNO₃

50 mL of 0.020 M Mg(OH)₂ titrated with 0.40 M HNO₃.

Note: Mg(OH)₂ → Mg²⁺ + 2 OH⁻ (ratio is 1:2 for base:acid)

a) Initial pH?

b) Volume of HNO₃ at equivalence?

a) Initial pH:
[OH⁻] = 2 × 0.020 = 0.040 M
pOH = −log(0.040) = 1.40
pH = 14.00 − 1.40 = 12.60

b) Volume at equivalence:
Mg(OH)₂ + 2 HNO₃ → Mg(NO₃)₂ + 2 H₂O
Veq = 0.050 L × 0.020 × (2/1) / 0.40 = 0.005 L = 5 mL HNO₃

pH at equivalence = 7 (SA + SB)
